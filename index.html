Full Professor of Statistics and Operations Research

Ntebogang Dinah Moroke

I develop statistical and computational methods that turn complex, noisy data into forecasts, risk measures and early-warning signals, across finance, energy, supply chains and health.

My methods draw on statistical mechanics, fractal analysis, wavelet signal processing, time-series econometrics and machine learning. I work with data from South Africa, Botswana, the BRICS economies, the United States and global cryptocurrency markets. My current focus is statistical mechanics and physics-informed methods.

I am based at North-West University, Mahikeng Campus, South Africa. I welcome conversations with researchers, organisations and students.

About

I am a statistician and operations researcher. I build statistical and computational methods for complex systems under stress, and statistical mechanics is my niche.

I have worked at North-West University since 2002 and have been a Full Professor since 2018. I studied there as well: a BCom in 2000, Honours in Statistics in 2001, an MCom in Statistics in 2005 and a PhD in Statistics in 2014.

Statistical mechanics gives a language for systems made of many interacting parts that are pushed away from equilibrium. Financial markets, power grids and supply chains are such systems. I use fractal analysis and wavelet methods to measure scaling and structure across time scales, and time-series econometrics and machine learning to forecast and to detect regime changes. I use physics-informed machine learning only where the physical structure of a problem adds something, and I test it against simpler benchmarks.

The applications are broad: financial markets and risk in South Africa, the BRICS economies, the United States and global cryptocurrency markets; electricity load, power-system dispatch and bioenergy; pharmaceutical supply chains; HIV/AIDS spatial statistics in Botswana; student attitudes and performance; retail and banking analytics; and sports science. One setting I return to often is the coupling between the JSE and Eskom load-shedding.

My current focus is narrowing to statistical mechanics and physics-informed methods, and I expect my work to centre there more and more.

Alongside research I have held academic leadership roles at North-West University: Head of Department (2007 to 2010), Programme Leader (2011 to 2014), Research Professor (2015 to 2017), Deputy Dean of the Faculty of Economic and Management Sciences (July 2017 to May 2026) and Acting Executive Dean (March 2021 to April 2022). Since May 2026 I have held no management post and give my time to research and supervision.

I have supervised nine doctoral and ten master's graduates to completion, and more than thirty Honours students. I received the Institutional Teaching Excellence Prestige Award in 2012, the Rector's Award for Most Productive Junior Researcher in 2015 and an NRF Knowledge Share Award in 2016.

I was principal investigator on a R2 million grant from the DST and CSIR (2020 to 2021) and a key collaborator on a three-year Erasmus+ Mobility Grant with Miskolc University (2022 to 2025). I share my datasets openly on Zenodo, and I serve as Guest Editor, journal reviewer and external examiner.

If your work involves forecasting, risk, early-warning or optimisation, see Work with me.

Full bio

Ntebogang Dinah Moroke is Full Professor of Statistics and Operations Research at North-West University, Mahikeng Campus, South Africa. She holds a PhD in Statistics from North-West University (2014) and has worked there since 2002, including as Head of Department, Programme Leader, Research Professor, Deputy Dean and Acting Executive Dean of the Faculty of Economic and Management Sciences. Her research applies statistical mechanics, fractal analysis, wavelet signal processing, time-series econometrics and machine learning to forecasting, risk and early warning in financial markets, energy systems, supply chains and health, using data from South Africa, Botswana, the BRICS economies, the United States and global markets. She has published in journals including IEEE Access, Entropy, Fractal and Fractional, Risks, Mathematics and the Frontiers series, shares open datasets on Zenodo, and has supervised nine doctoral and ten master's graduates. She is Guest Editor of a Fractal and Fractional Special Issue and a peer reviewer for international journals.

Research

Complex systems behave differently under stress. My research builds methods to measure that change and to give early warning of it, in settings where data are noisy and decisions matter.

Statistical mechanics

Non-equilibrium statistical mechanics applied to time series from markets, power systems and supply chains.

Fractal analysis

Scaling, memory and complexity in market behaviour.

Wavelet signal processing

Separating structure across time scales in noisy market and infrastructure signals.

Physics-informed machine learning

Used where the physical structure of a problem adds something, and benchmarked against simpler methods.

Where I apply it

The methods carry across fields. Published applications use data from South Africa, Botswana, the BRICS economies, the United States and global cryptocurrency markets.

Finance and risk

Volatility and regime detection on the JSE, BRICS exchange rates and stock markets, cryptocurrency liquidity and portfolio risk, and downside-risk and inflation forecasting.

Energy

Electricity-load and wind-power forecasting, power-system dispatch under load-shedding, and bioenergy grids. Load-shedding is a recurring setting, including its effect on financial markets.

Supply chains

Spectral and graph methods for detecting connectivity collapse, and for validating explainable AI, in pharmaceutical supply chains. Two published papers.

Health and social data

Spatial statistics for HIV/AIDS in Botswana, structural equation models of adolescent behaviour, and student attitudes and performance.

Business analytics

Statistical arbitrage in the retail sector, fraud detection in banking, household debt, and employee turnover and performance.

Sports science

Fatigue classification in sprint and jump athletes.

Work with me

My methods turn constrained, noisy, real-world data into forecasts, early-warning signals and decisions. I welcome conversations with researchers, organisations and students.

Forecasting and risk

Market, electricity-load and downside-risk forecasting, regime detection and early-warning signals, in published work on the JSE, BRICS exchange rates, gold, mining and cryptocurrency series.

Energy and infrastructure

Power-system dispatch and optimisation, and how load-shedding feeds through to markets. Published in IEEE Access, IET Renewable Power Generation and the Frontiers series.

Supply chains and operations

Spectral and graph methods for detecting connectivity collapse and validating explainable AI, applied to pharmaceutical supply chains in two published papers.

Health, education and social data

Spatial statistics for HIV/AIDS, structural equation models of behaviour, and analysis of student attitudes and performance.

Research training

Co-supervision of master's and doctoral students. Nine doctoral and ten master's students have completed under my supervision.

Ways to work together

  • Research collaboration: co-authored papers, joint grant applications and shared datasets.

  • Students and visitors: co-supervision, and visiting scholars and students.
  • Applied roles: advisory, part-time or full-time positions where applied statistics, forecasting and operations research are needed, in any industry.
